  R82 58, R83 17, R84 14, R85 3, R86 7, R87 8, R88 3, R89 16, R90 2, R91 27, R92 7, R93 7, R94 11,
  R95 11, R96 4, R97 30, R98 42, R99 9, R101 6, R102 2, R103 47. Target for the final build: site
  ACPL <= 15, CPL <= 8 cp.
  2. Time is the cheapest edge. The top five think 1.26-1.54 s per move (max 6.3-14.9 s) and leave
  22-44 s unused, mostly by spending a fixed 3-4% of the remaining clock. A budget that spends 2-3x
  that in the middlegame (about 3-5 s on moves 10-40, more after an evaluation swing) and keeps a 10
  s reserve buys one to two extra plies on every critical move without any risk of flagging; the 0.5
  s increment covers a long endgame on its own.
  3. Draw policy: the top five gave away 1 half points by repeating while ahead, but accepted 67
  level repetitions. So: positive contempt against everyone below the top ten (they hand out half
  points; see RepA in the summary), near-zero contempt against the top three when we are not better,
  and a repetition-seeking mode when we are worse against them: they will take it.
  4. There are no swindles at the top: 134 of 144 positions at +300 were converted. A game against
  them is decided by the first -300, so the evaluation must be right in the middlegame structures
  the curated set produces (Sicilian Closed, English Symmetrical, Ruy Lopez Closed, Petroff,
  Caro-Kann) rather than by endgame technique.
  5. Tactics only pay against the teams that blunder from level positions: c1 (5.6/1000), Challenger
  (4.3/1000), BINI Alpha (4.0/1000). Against class-A engines keep the position sound and let the
  time edge and the endgame do the work.
  6. Clock pressure works against AlphaFish (3 games under 5 s), c1 (46 games under 5 s), stockfih
  (8 games under 5 s), ms (31 games under 5 s), test_bot (54 games under 5 s): keep those games long
  and complicated, never simplify early against them.
  7. Openings: a shipped book is low value (231 curated positions, the top 40 cover a third of
  games, and every game leaves theory within a few moves). What matters is a cheap, correct first
  move out of the curated position (a 1-2 s search is fine) and an evaluation that understands
  closed Sicilian, symmetrical English and closed Ruy Lopez structures.

  1. Move quality first.  Our v1 sat at site ACPL 82; the two v2 ladder games so far scored 4 and
     23; the arena says v2 beats Stockfish-2400 12/12 at the real clock.  The number that decides
     the final is CPL against the site's evaluations: the podium is at 6.5-8.3, the rest of the top
     ten at 10-18.  Judge every eval or search change by this number on the curated positions.

     thinks longer after a big eval change) and a 10 s floor gives us one to two extra plies on the
     moves that decide games, with zero flag risk given the 0.5 s increment.  Against AlphaFish and
     Capablanca this is the only structural edge available: they are playing 1.4-1.6 s a move by
     move 40.  Our own v2 left 95 s unused in R31; fix that before anything else.
  3. Repetitions, two regimes.  On the ladder (since 6 Sept ~11:00) a threefold or fifty-move
     ending voids the game and it is re-rated as if never played: when worse, a repetition is a

     contempt whenever we are better, and an active repetition hunt whenever we are worse, against
     everyone.  The top five accepted 23 level repetitions and 3 while ahead, and AlphaFish plays
     instantly once it holds a mate score, so the undo is often on offer.  For the final Swiss the
     published rules still say FIDE draws; keep the contempt setting switchable and confirm with

     first -300, so the evaluation in closed Sicilian, symmetrical English, closed Ruy Lopez and
     Petroff structures matters more than anything in the opening.
