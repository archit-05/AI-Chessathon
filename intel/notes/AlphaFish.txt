Both visible flaws are endgame technique, not search.  (1) The only loss, R10 as Black vs Oyinda:
+9.6 with bishop and pawns against connected passers, it allowed 48.c8=Q and played 48...a5??
instead of 48...Bxc8 with 30 s on the clock: a promotion or board-sync bug in that early build.
(2) The R32 draw as Black vs zak (rank 17): a queen and a knight up, a forced mate found at own
move 55 but not played, then every move from 58 on took 2-14 ms (Qf3+, Qd5+, Qd6+, Qd4, Qd2+, Qd4,
Nd5) until threefold repetition with +9991 on the board and 27 s left.  Once its search returns
a mate score it plays instantly and does not avoid repeating positions.
Use: we will usually be the lost side.  Never simplify into a forced mate; keep the king running
and give the checks that recreate positions, and let its mate-score behaviour hand over the half
point.  Otherwise it is the best engine on the ladder (CPL 6.5, 0.1% blunders, unbeaten vs the
top ten), has no book, and spends a fixed ~4% of the remaining clock per move (4.4 s at move 1,
~1.5 s by move 40), so long games lower its depth while ours need not fall.
