What the top of the ladder actually is (6 Sept, after round 31)
- Three systems play at near-engine level: AlphaFish (site ACPL 16, our CPL 6 at 120k nodes,
  unbeaten against the top ten: 7 wins 3 draws), Capablanca (16 / 10) and Mate in One (16 / 10).
  SoberJackson and Anchoa are half a class behind (17-20 / 12), then a pack at 20-23.  Every one
  of them is a classical alpha-beta searcher: no book (only Anchoa plays a fifth of its early
  moves instantly), no contempt games, nothing exotic in the openings, and a clock policy that
  spends a fixed 3-4% of what is left, so they play 4 s early and 1-1.5 s by move 40 and finish
  with 25-45 s unused.  Their strength is uniformly good move quality, not depth bursts.
- Nobody at the top is beatable by tricks: 0 repetitions accepted while ahead in the top five,
  every +300 converted, and no time trouble.  AlphaFish's only loss was a promotion bug in its
  first build, since fixed.  The mid-table (ranks 6-20) is different: AI Fellow, test_bot and
  Mate in One do drop under 5 s, Danya's Disciple and make_no_mistakes fade after move 40, and
  AI Fellow has already given a half point by repeating while ahead.

How we beat them (engineering, in priority order)
1. Move quality first.  Our v1 sat at site ACPL 82; the arena says v2 beats Stockfish-2400 12/12
   at the real clock.  The dossier number that decides the final is the 120k-node CPL: the
   podium is at 6-10 cp, the rest of the top ten at 12-22.  Every eval or search improvement
   should be judged by this number on the same 300 curated positions the site uses.
2. Spend the clock they do not.  A 3-5 s middlegame budget with a swing bonus (they never think
   longer after a big eval change) and a 10 s floor gives us one to two extra plies on the moves
   that decide games, with zero flag risk given the 0.5 s increment.  Against AlphaFish this is
   the only structural edge available: it is playing 1.4 s a move by move 40.
3. Repetition policy by opponent tier: positive contempt below the top ten (they hand out draws),
   zero against the top three when level, and actively seek repetition when worse against them
   (all five top teams accept level repetitions).  Never repeat while ahead ourselves: we did it
   three times in the v1 games and it cost us against weaker opponents.
4. Do not build for swindles: nobody at the top fails to convert.  A game against a class-A
   engine is lost at the first -300, so the middlegame evaluation in closed Sicilian, symmetrical
   English, closed Ruy Lopez and Petroff structures matters more than endgame technique.
5. Skip the opening book: 231 curated positions so far, the most common seen five times, and the
   top 40 cover a third of games.  A correct 1-2 s search out of the curated position is enough.
