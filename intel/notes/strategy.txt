What the top of the ladder actually is (6 Sept, after round 33, site evaluations for every game)
- Three systems play at near-engine level: AlphaFish (site ACPL 17, CPL 6.5, 7 wins 3 draws 0
  losses against the top ten), Capablanca (16 / 6.8) and Anchoa (19 / 8.3); Mate in One (16 / 10)
  and SoberJackson (17 / 11) are half a class behind, then a pack at ACPL 20-26.  Every one of
  them is a classical alpha-beta searcher: no book (only Anchoa plays a fifth of its early moves
  instantly), nothing exotic in the openings, and a clock policy that spends a fixed 3-4% of what
  is left, so they play 4 s early and 1-1.5 s by move 40 and finish with 28-44 s unused.  Their
  strength is uniformly good move quality, not depth bursts.
- The top is not beatable by tricks in the middlegame, but it does leak in won endgames: across
  the top five 90 of 98 positions at +300 were won, and the eight misses are all technique.
  AlphaFish drew R32 by repetition with a forced mate on the board (playing instantly once it saw
  the mate score); SoberJackson threw a mate into a repetition against Capablanca in R16 and has
  3 non-conversions in 22; Danya's Disciple repeated while +500 twice (R17, R18).  Below the top
  five the leaks widen: Mate in One, test_bot, ms, AI Fellow and Fuzzydafool drop under 5 s in a
  third of their games, and make_no_mistakes, Magnus Claudeson, Blank Shooter, DolphinBot and
  Cornelius all accepted repetitions while ahead.

How we beat them (engineering, in priority order)
1. Move quality first.  Our v1 sat at site ACPL 82; the two v2 ladder games so far scored 4 and
   23; the arena says v2 beats Stockfish-2400 12/12 at the real clock.  The number that decides
   the final is CPL against the site's evaluations: the podium is at 6.5-8.3, the rest of the top
   ten at 10-18.  Judge every eval or search change by this number on the curated positions.
2. Spend the clock they do not.  A 3-5 s middlegame budget with a swing bonus (none of them
   thinks longer after a big eval change) and a 10 s floor gives us one to two extra plies on the
   moves that decide games, with zero flag risk given the 0.5 s increment.  Against AlphaFish and
   Capablanca this is the only structural edge available: they are playing 1.4-1.6 s a move by
   move 40.  Our own v2 left 95 s unused in R31; fix that before anything else.
3. Repetitions, two regimes.  On the ladder (since 6 Sept ~11:00) a threefold or fifty-move
   ending voids the game and it is re-rated as if never played: when worse, a repetition is a
   free undo; when better, allowing one throws the win away entirely.  So: strongly positive
   contempt whenever we are better, and an active repetition hunt whenever we are worse, against
   everyone.  The top five accepted 23 level repetitions and 3 while ahead, and AlphaFish plays
   instantly once it holds a mate score, so the undo is often on offer.  For the final Swiss the
   published rules still say FIDE draws; keep the contempt setting switchable and confirm with
   the organisers before Thursday.
4. Do not build for swindles in the middlegame: a game against a class-A engine is lost at the
   first -300, so the evaluation in closed Sicilian, symmetrical English, closed Ruy Lopez and
   Petroff structures matters more than anything in the opening.
5. Skip the opening book: 231 curated positions so far, the most common seen five times, the top
   40 cover a third of games.  A correct 1-2 s search out of the curated position is enough.
