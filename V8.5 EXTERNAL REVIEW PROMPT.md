## 4. Measurement methodology — and the traps we have already fallen into

These were learned expensively. Please assume they hold.

1. **Fixed-node gates inflate eval gains ~2–3×.** Measured survival from a 30k-node screen to a real
   clock: ft_mid **+25 → +9**; fc_b **+29 → +13.7**; aspiration-18 **+14 → −3**. Fixed nodes remains
   a cheap, deterministic, contention-immune *screen*, but nothing ships on it.
2. **Never read a partial gate.** Six times in one night a partial pointed somewhere the full sample
   did not go, always in the exciting direction.
3. **Test the difference, not two point estimates.** Two arms measured in separate gates have √2×
   the uncertainty of either. A 34-Elo gap between arms carrying ±35 each means nothing.
4. **One 400-game gate at 120+0.5 cannot resolve 20 Elo** — the draw rate is ~84% in self-play. Two
   identical 400-game gates on the same net returned **+23 and +1**.
5. **Self-play understates any policy that depends on being stronger** (contempt, draw-avoidance).
   Such levers must be gated against a *weaker* opponent — and that opponent must be the **same net
   width**, or the speed difference biases a timed gate.
6. **`node_gate` (our fixed-node harness) cannot A/B time-management constants** — its `_budget` is a
   module global applied to both sides. Those need `arena_par`, the real wrapper.
7. **Check the harness's own diagnostics.** An arena run at 40 workers blew `agent.py`'s 75 s
   compile-join and played 50 games on the *fallback* (non-NNUE) engine, printing a perfectly
   plausible 50.0%. `BINI_JOIN_S=300` and a fallback count of 0 are now mandatory.
8. **Profile data distribution before training on it.** Seeding 38% of a fine-tune corpus from
   endgame/late-ply positions cost **21 Elo**; label-correctness checks all passed.

## 5. What has been established about the current build

### 5.1 Confirmed defects (reproduced and measured)

**A. The engine cannot recognise a categorically drawn ending.** Measured on the live build at
depth 21–22:

| position | V8 evaluates | truth |
|---|---|---|
| K+B vs K | **+85** | 0 |
| K+B vs K+N | **+81** | 0 |
| K+N vs K | **+79** | 0 |
| K+2B vs K (winnable) | −1326 | large ✓ |

Cause: `endgame_scale` fires only on pawnless boards and **divides by 8 rather than returning 0**;
+500 ÷ 8 = +62, which still reads as better than an equal position with pieces on.
**Direct cause of R99**: the engine held **+60 to +150 for 64 moves**, then deliberately traded into
King+Bishop vs King, scoring that move **+80**. It did not blunder — it played correctly on a false
number. Also plausibly explains R87, R88, R90 (all "drawn by insufficient material").

**B. The opening book was chosen by an engine that no longer exists.** `book.json` is byte-identical
across V6.5, V7k and V8 (md5 `51c97f3f…`, built 9 Sept 11:58) while the net was replaced twice
(+39, then +17 Elo). On the positions where the book actually fired in rounds 91–98 it plays a
**different move from the current net in 12 of 22 cases (55%)**. In **R98** (a loss) **three of four
book moves were ones this net rejects**; the evaluation walked **+22 → −46** during the book phase
and we left book at **−70**, the worst start in the record.

**C. The endgame is searched ~3 plies shallower than the middlegame.** `agent.py::_budget` has
`mtg = max(18.0, 34.0 - 0.4*move_number)` — pinned at 18 from move 40 onward, so the engine assumes
18 moves always remain and spends 1/18 of its clock every move, front-loaded. Our game lengths are
43–106 moves. In R98 it reached move 73 of 89 with **11.5 s**, playing at 0.5–1.0 s, **depth 16–17
against a middlegame median of 20**. (A simulation of the formula predicted 10.9 s; actual 11.5 s.)

**D. The transposition table returns wrong scores in repetition-adjacent positions.** R94 move 42
reproduced exactly: **warm TT +383 at depth 16, cold TT −20 at depth 15**, same position, same net.
Almost certainly the classic TT-vs-repetition interaction (repetition is path-dependent, TT entries
are not). **However: warm or cold, at 1.5 s or 60 s, the engine plays the same move (Ke2).** A wrong
number, not a wrong move — so far.

### 5.2 Hypotheses we formed and then killed with our own tests

- **Contempt / draw-seeking** as a draw-conversion lever. Closed across four instruments and ~4,000
  games: contempt 0 and 20 both score +68 vs a weaker net; 45 scores +46; draw-seek-when-behind
  costs 12; at a real clock against a weaker opponent contempt 45 is **14 Elo worse** than the
  shipped 20. Two earlier static-eval attempts (anti-simplification, drawishness) also measured dead.
- **The quiet-position time discount** (`stable>=3 → 0.75×`) as the cause of R100's loss. Refuted:
  at the losing move, at **1,742 ms / 3,106 ms / 6,832 ms / 20,000 ms** (depths 14→23) the engine
  plays **the same move every time**. More time does not change it.
- **Endgame clock reserve.** `mtg` floor 18→24 gated **−8 ±34**; floor 18→30 gated **−1 ±34**
  (400 games each, real wrapper, 120+0.5, zero fallbacks). **No effect.**
- **MIDGAME_BOOST 1.25** (spend more in moves 16–35): **+11 ±46**, null.
- **Aspiration window.** A clean five-point single peak on the fixed-node screen (12: −22, **18:
  +14**, 25: shipped, 35: −14, 50: −14, all 1728 games) measured **−3 ±17 at a real clock.** Closed.
- **Net training.** Recipe axis closed (lr/batch peak worth ~+9, already banked). Width closed (1536:
  −27, 2048: −4 at fixed nodes, which is an *upper* bound for a wider net). From-scratch on depth-10
  data impossible before the deadline on epoch arithmetic (a superbatch is a fixed 100M positions, so
  41M rows = 236 epochs → **−345**; 470 epochs → **−366**). Endgame-blended corpora monotonically
  negative (0% → +29, 10% → +19, 20% → +12, 38% → +4 on the screen).

### 5.3 Gate results for the five V8.5 candidates (all vs V8, real wrapper, 120+0.5, 0 fallbacks)

| candidate | change | result |
|---|---|---|
| mtg30 | endgame clock reserve, floor 30 | **−1 ±34** (400 games, FINAL) |
| mtg24 | endgame clock reserve, floor 24 | **−8 ±34** (400 games, FINAL) |
| insuf | K+B vs K etc. score 0, not +62 | **−0 ±37** (349/400) |
| book | rebuilt with the current net | **−17 ±37** (340/400) |
| stable | remove quiet-position 0.75× discount | −0 ±73 (90/400) |

**Not one repair has demonstrated a gain.** The rebuilt book — the change with the strongest prior
evidence — is currently the worst performer.

Note on the rebuilt book: it has **100% start-position coverage vs the old book's 93.3%** (the old
book had *no* prepared move for 82 of 1,226 start positions), 7,566 entries vs 6,105, and 65% move
agreement with the old one.

Note on the insuf gate: **60 of 349 games ended by insufficient material**, so the fix had ample
opportunity to act and did not change the rate. Our current reading is that a V8.5-vs-V8 gate is
structurally blind to it — both engines are near-identical, so those endings arise from *equal*
positions where declining them wins nothing, whereas the fix is designed for the case where we are
**+150 and liquidate anyway**. A weaker-opponent comparison is now running.
